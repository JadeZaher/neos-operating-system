# commitment-unwinding

## C. Trigger Conditions

- **Voluntary exit initiated**: the voluntary-exit skill generates a commitment inventory and triggers this skill for systematic resolution
- **ETHOS dissolution initiated**: the ethos-dissolution skill triggers commitment unwinding for all members of the dissolving unit
- **Partial departure**: a member leaves one circle or ETHOS but remains in the ecosystem, requiring unwinding of commitments specific to that unit
- **Role reassignment**: when a major role reassignment requires unwinding the outgoing holder's accumulated commitments (even without ecosystem departure)

## D. Required Inputs

- **Commitment inventory**: the complete, verified list of the departing member's commitments, generated by the voluntary-exit skill and organized into five categories
- **Departure timeline**: the agreed handoff period (7-day urgent or 30-day standard) that constrains resolution deadlines
- **Receiving party candidates**: a list of potential recipients for each transferable commitment, identified by the departure coordinator in consultation with circle stewards
- **Agreement registry data**: current agreement texts and party lists for all agreements involving the departing member
- **Resource allocation ledger**: current balances and earmarks for all economic commitments involving the departing member

## E. Step-by-Step Process

1. **Categorize each commitment.** The departure coordinator sorts the commitment inventory into five categories: role obligations, agreement obligations, economic commitments, pending proposals, and active conflict resolutions. Each commitment is tagged with its urgency (blocking, time-sensitive, or routine) and complexity (simple transfer, requires consent, or requires restructuring).
2. **Unwind role obligations.** For each role the departing member holds: identify a willing successor through the circle or role-assignment process, conduct a knowledge transfer during the handoff period, and formally reassign the role. If no successor is available, the role enters the emergency-transfer queue and the circle distributes critical functions among remaining members. Sole critical roles trigger graceful degradation protocols.
3. **Unwind agreement obligations.** For each agreement: determine whether the departing member can be removed as a party (with consent from remaining parties per the agreement-creation skill), whether a replacement party should be added, or whether the agreement must be amended to reflect the departure. Agreements where the departing member is the sole remaining party are closed and archived.
4. **Unwind economic commitments.** For each resource allocation: unspent earmarked funds return to the source pool, shared resource stewardship transfers to a named successor, and any outstanding debts or contributions are settled. Current-See allocations that have not been exercised are returned. Allocations already committed to specific projects transfer to the project steward.
5. **Unwind pending proposals.** For each proposal the departing member authored that is still in process: the member may withdraw the proposal, transfer authorship to a co-author or willing member, or allow it to proceed with a notation that the author has departed. Proposals in active ACT consent rounds are paused for 7 days to allow authorship transfer before defaulting to withdrawal.
6. **Unwind active conflict resolutions.** For each conflict resolution process involving the departing member: if the member is a disputant, the process pauses for 14 days to allow the remaining party to decide whether to continue with a substitute or close the matter. If the member is a mediator or facilitator, a replacement is assigned through the conflict resolution skill (Layer VI). Conflict records are preserved regardless of departure.
7. **Obtain receiving party acknowledgment.** For every transferred commitment, the receiving party explicitly acknowledges acceptance. Acknowledgments are recorded in the Commitment Unwinding Ledger. No commitment is considered resolved without either a receiving party acknowledgment or an emergency-queue entry.
8. **Compile Commitment Unwinding Ledger.** The departure coordinator assembles the ledger using `assets/commitment-unwinding-ledger-template.yaml`, documenting every commitment, its resolution method, the receiving party, and the acknowledgment status. The ledger is attached to the Departure Record.

## F. Output Artifact

A Commitment Unwinding Ledger following `assets/commitment-unwinding-ledger-template.yaml`. The ledger contains: ledger ID, departing member identity, departure timeline, and an entry for each commitment showing: commitment type, description, resolution method (transfer, close, return, withdraw, pause), receiving party identity, acknowledgment status, and completion date. The ledger also records any commitments that entered the emergency-transfer queue with the reason they could not be resolved within the departure timeline.

## G. Authority Boundary Check

- **The departing member** participates in unwinding but cannot be compelled to fulfill commitments beyond the departure timeline
- **The departure coordinator** facilitates resolution but cannot unilaterally reassign commitments without receiving party consent
- **Circle stewards** identify potential receiving parties within their circles but cannot force anyone to accept a transferred commitment
- **No body or individual** can add new commitments to a departing member's inventory after the commitment inventory is verified
- **The ecosystem** bears responsibility for commitments it cannot reassign -- orphaned commitments are the ecosystem's problem, not the departing member's
- **OSC** may be consulted for ecosystem-level commitment transfers but does not have veto authority over the unwinding process

## H. Capture Resistance Check

**Obligation inflation.** The commitment inventory is generated from formal records (agreement-registry, role-assignment, resource ledger), not from informal claims. No one can add unrecorded "commitments" to delay departure. The inventory is verified by the departing member, who can contest any item they do not recognize.

**Guilt-based retention.** The structured, category-by-category process replaces emotional appeals with procedural steps. The coordinator -- drawn from outside the member's circle -- processes each commitment mechanically. There is no "are you sure?" checkpoint, no retention conversation embedded in the unwinding process.

**Hostage commitments.** No single commitment can extend the departure timeline beyond 30 days. If a commitment genuinely cannot be resolved in 30 days (e.g., a multi-year project), the commitment transfers to the ecosystem with a transition plan. The departing member walks away clean on day 30 regardless.

**Selective enforcement.** The same unwinding process applies to every departing member, regardless of their role, seniority, or social standing. A founding member's commitments are unwound with the same template as a new member's.

## I. Failure Containment Logic

- **No receiving party available for a role**: the role enters the emergency-transfer queue, and the circle distributes critical functions across remaining members until a permanent replacement is found through role-assignment
- **Agreement parties refuse to consent to amendment**: the departing member's participation is annotated as "departed -- inactive party" in the agreement registry, and the agreement is flagged for review at its next scheduled review date
- **Economic settlement dispute**: if the departing member and ecosystem disagree on the amount owed or to be returned, the dispute is escalated to Layer VI conflict resolution with a 14-day expedited timeline; the member's departure is not delayed
- **Departing member becomes unreachable**: the coordinator completes unwinding unilaterally using best-available information, noting "member unreachable" in the ledger for each affected commitment
- **Commitment inventory contains errors**: the departing member may contest inventory items at any point during the process; contested items are verified against source records and corrected

## J. Expiry / Review Condition

The Commitment Unwinding Ledger does not expire -- it is a permanent governance record attached to the Departure Record. The unwinding process itself is reviewed annually through the ACT consent process to ensure the five commitment categories remain comprehensive and the resolution protocols remain practical. If a new type of commitment emerges that does not fit existing categories (e.g., a new economic instrument), the unwinding protocols are updated through an agreement amendment. Emergency-queue items are reviewed weekly by the ETHOS governance facilitator until resolved.

## K. Exit Compatibility Check

This skill is a core component of the exit process -- it is triggered by voluntary-exit and feeds its output into the Departure Record. The skill ensures that no commitment can block exit: every category has a resolution path that completes within the departure timeline, with emergency-queue fallback for anything unresolved. The Commitment Unwinding Ledger becomes part of the departing member's portable record if they choose to include it.

## L. Cross-Unit Interoperability Impact

When a member departs a single ETHOS but remains in the ecosystem, only commitments scoped to that ETHOS are unwound. Ecosystem-level commitments (e.g., participation in cross-ETHOS agreements) remain active. When a member departs the entire ecosystem, all commitments across all ETHOS are unwound in a coordinated process. The ledger template is standardized across all NEOS ecosystems, enabling a receiving ecosystem to understand how commitments were resolved during a previous departure. Cross-ETHOS role transfers (e.g., transferring a role from SHUR Bali to SHUR Costa Rica) follow the same unwinding protocol with cross-unit coordinator consultation.
